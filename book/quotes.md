# Quotes for A Library

Collected while making the book (2026-11-26). Not printed unless they're
named in book.toml. Sources checked as far as possible; read the notes
before quoting.

## In the book

**Terry Pratchett, *Soul Music* (1994).** Runs across the top of the shelf
(running.txt). Check the wording against a copy before printing.

> “The library didn’t only contain magical books, the ones which are
> chained to the shelves and are very dangerous. It also contained
> perfectly ordinary books, printed on commonplace paper in mundane ink. It
> would be a mistake to think that they weren’t also dangerous just because
> reading them didn’t make fireworks go off in the sky. Reading them
> sometimes did the more dangerous trick of making fireworks go off in the
> privacy of the reader’s brain.”

## Not used (yet)

**The antilibrary.** Pasted as: "Italian novelist Umberto Eco famously
argued that a personal library should contain far more unread books than
read ones. Known as an antilibrary, this collection serves as a daily
reminder of the vastness of human knowledge—acting as a research tool and
an antidote to intellectual arrogance rather than a vanity display of
finished titles."
*Note:* the word "antilibrary" is Nassim Nicholas Taleb's, in *The Black
Swan* (2007), describing Eco's library of about 30,000 books. Eco didn't
argue it under that name. Credit Taleb for the term.

**Arthur Schopenhauer, *Parerga and Paralipomena* (1851).** Pasted as:
"buying books would be a good thing if one could also buy the time to read
them; people too often mistake the purchase of a book for the acquisition
of its contents." A real passage; quote it from a translation, not this
paraphrase.

**Tsundoku.** Japanese: acquiring reading material and letting it pile up
unread. A real word. The pasted gloss ("a wine cellar of ideas, waiting for
the exact right moment in your life to uncork a specific voice") is
someone's phrasing, not a source.

**"So many books, so little time."** Usually credited to Frank Zappa, with
no known source; "attributed to" if ever used. Cut 2026-11-26.
